Research

Gender, resources, and unequal pathways

I examine how family resources, institutions, and working arrangements are associated with gender inequality across education and employment.

Family resources and gendered educational pathways

How do economic resources and institutional advantages shape aspirations, entry into STEM, and the realization of educational credentials? My research distinguishes household income, wealth, housing, and parental education, and examines educational pathways in Germany and China alongside cross-national patterns.

The German research uses NEPS to follow students from secondary education into higher education. Comparative research uses TIMSS to examine economic security, STEM returns, and women’s access to STEM occupations.

  • Does Affluence Widen the Gender Gap in STEM Choices? Micro-Level Evidence from GermanySubmitted after major revision
  • Economic Opportunity and the Formation of Gendered STEM Aspirations: Security, Returns, and AccessibilityUnder external review
  • From Housing Institutions to Gendered Fields of Study: The Educational Conversion of Institutional Advantage in Marketizing ChinaWork in progress
  • Assets beyond Housing and Gendered Entry into Science and Engineering among Homeowning Families in ChinaWith editor

Platform and informal work

How do platform rules, occupational expectations, and employment protection structure women’s opportunities and earnings? This research examines entry into gig work, gender wage gaps, and women drivers’ experiences of evaluation, safety, and algorithmic management in China.

I combine labor-market survey analysis with semi-structured interviews. The interview research examines workers’ interpretations of platform protection and their efforts to negotiate workable conditions.

  • Algorithmic Benevolent Sexism: Female Ride-Hailing Drivers and the Paradox of Protection in ChinaSubmitted after minor revision
  • Algorithmic Collaboration and Negotiated Dependence: Gendered Claim-Making and Platform Governance in China’s Ride-Hailing SectorUnder external review
  • Platform Access and Conditional Occupational Membership among Women Ride-Hailing Drivers in ChinaWith editor
  • Flexible but Penalized? Gender Wage Gaps in China’s Informal SectorWith editor

Household and couple inequality

Related work examines how parental education is associated with partner matching and how employment protection relates to wage inequality within couples. These studies connect family background, labor-market position, and the distribution of resources within households.

  • More College–College Couples Without a Stronger College Boundary? Population Composition and Parental-Education Sorting in ChinaWork in progress
  • Employment Protection and Within-Couple Wage Inequality: Evidence from ChinaWith editor

Data and methods

My work uses longitudinal and comparative survey data, including NEPS, TIMSS, and the China Labor-force Dynamics Survey. Methods include regression analysis, wage decomposition, selection and distributional models, and qualitative analysis of semi-structured interviews.
